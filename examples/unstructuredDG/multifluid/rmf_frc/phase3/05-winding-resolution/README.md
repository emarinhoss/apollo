# 05 — does the antenna instability go away when the winding is resolved?

## The question

`03-formation` dies at 1.73 of its 20 RMF periods, and everything grows at the
antenna winding (`docs/known-issues.md` §19). What grows is a grid-scale
charge band, growing exponentially and pinned to the mesh's stitching seams,
with an O(1) error in Gauss's law at the winding. The leading explanation is a
charge-separation layer the mesh cannot resolve: the shipped mesh has 0.29 cells
per Debye length at the winding.

Both earlier refinement tests stayed below one cell per Debye length, so
neither could tell. This ladder crosses it. Separately, one arm rotates the
mesh to test whether the seams are the site.

## The arms

All five are the shipped antenna deck, unchanged except for the mesh and the
run length. Frames are written every 1/24 of a period in every arm, so frame k
is at the same physical time in all of them.

| deck | mesh | λ_D/h at the winding | triangles | dt [s] | run to | steps | one rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `reference` | the shipped mesh, regenerated byte for byte | 0.29 | 11,989 | 3.572e-11 | 1 period | 35,212 | 7 h |
| `ring40` | refined region extended to r = 0.040 | 0.40 | 15,013 | 3.545e-11 | 1 period | 35,487 | 9 h |
| `fine40` | as ring40, h-inner 6.0e-4 | 0.62 | 31,948 | 2.275e-11 | 1 period | 55,293 | 29 h |
| `resolved40` | as ring40, h-inner 3.5e-4 | **1.06** | 87,834 | 1.337e-11 | 0.5 period | 47,041 | 68 h |
| `rotated60` | the shipped mesh turned by 60° | 0.29 | 11,989 | 3.572e-11 | 0.5 period | 17,606 | 3.5 h |

- **λ_D/h** is measured, not nominal. It is λ_D = 4.07e-4 m over the mean
  edge length of triangles centred in the winding, 0.0335–0.0385. The same
  measure reproduces §19's figures for the shipped mesh exactly. Mean edges run
  about 1.1 times the generator's target size, which is why `resolved40` uses
  3.5e-4 rather than 4.0e-4: the latter measures only 0.93.
- **dt** is what the solver chose in a local smoke run, and it matches the cost
  model to every printed figure.
- **Hours** are the cost model's. A local smoke run on one pinned core
  measured 0.72, 0.91, 1.90 and 5.1 s/step for the first four arms, against
  the model's 0.71, 0.89, 1.90 and 5.2. Your node will differ; the steps will
  not.
- **`rotated60`** has identical cells and an identical time step to
  `reference`. Rotation is an exact symmetry of this problem, so the only thing
  that differs is where the mesh's seams fall.

The meshes are generated by `scripts/rmf_make_phase3_runs.py` through
`scripts/mkdiscmesh.py`, and `--check` regenerates them and compares byte for
byte.

## Running it

```bash
sbatch --array=0-4 slurm_array.sh 05-winding-resolution
```

Decks are taken in sorted order: 0 `fine40`, 1 `reference`, 2 `resolved40`,
3 `ring40`, 4 `rotated60`. Each task is one rank on one pinned core. That is
not a preference: the answer depends on the rank count (§15).

`resolved40` needs about 68 hours. If your queue's limit is shorter, let it be
killed and resubmit the same array. `slurm_array.sh` sets `APOLLO_RESUME=1`, so
each task continues from its last frame, and a task that already finished
is reported complete rather than run again.

Output is about 1.2 GB across the five arms. That is an estimate from frame
size per cell, not a measurement.

## Reading the result

From the repository root, for each arm's results folder:

```bash
python3 scripts/ring_spectrum.py --bands winding <deck.pin> <frames...>   # grid-band growth rate
python3 scripts/winding_anatomy.py <deck.pin> <frames...>                 # Gauss residual, electron speeds
python3 scripts/where_peak.py <deck.pin> <frames...>                      # where each component peaks
```

Frames are named after the deck's `Simulation` key, `frc2d_<n>.vtu`.

**The decision rule.** It is written down before the runs, so the runs cannot
move it:

1. **Resolution is the cure.** The grid band of the charge density, ρ_c, on the
   winding rings grows more slowly at each step up the ladder. At
   `resolved40` it is gone, or slower than `reference` by more than the
   factor-5.6 scatter §19 measured across its uniform ladder. The Gauss
   residual at the winding falls with it. Then the instability is numerical,
   and resolving it costs about 137 hours per period on one rank (twice this
   arm's 68 h), which is not viable for a 20-period run.
   The next step is a solver fix: damping on the cleaning potential, a common
   flux speed for the two species, or a positivity-preserving limiter.
2. **Resolution changes nothing.** The rates are within that scatter across
   all four arms, and the residual does not fall. Then the growth is physical,
   or the model's own behaviour, and the deck is what has to change.
3. **The mesh is the site.** In `rotated60` the peaks sit about 60° from
   where they sit in `reference`, which is 0° and ±120° there. Then the
   stitching seams are where it starts, whichever of 1 or 2 holds, and
   `mkdiscmesh.py`'s seam quality is worth fixing on its own.

Anything between 1 and 2 is not a verdict. Say so, and do not round it to one.
